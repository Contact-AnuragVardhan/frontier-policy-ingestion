Universal School Choice

See which programs meet EdChoice’s standard for universal educational freedom

Milton and Rose Friedman envisioned a future where every family could choose the learning environment that best fits their child. Achieving that vision requires more than simply creating school choice programs. A truly universal program must meet three standards:

All Students — Every student is eligible.

All Options — Families have broad flexibility in how they may use education funds.

All Dollars — Programs are funded through predictable, sustainable revenue streams.

Universal Options

The states below allow families wide flexibility in how they may spend program funds. EdChoice considered the 15 most common educational expenses, and states permitting a majority of those uses are considered to have programs with universal options. Programs that require families to spend on tuition first were excluded, unless the state offers multiple programs that can work in partnership to achieve this standard. Eighteen states have programs with universal options.

Universal Funding

Programs considered universally funded are those where the non-capped source of funding is consistent and sustainable and either provided through the state’s funding formula, education budget, general revenue streams, or an ongoing appropriation with an automatic escalator provision to meet demand. Nineteen states plus Puerto Rico have programs with universal funding.
